# Working Notes — Traditional Food Niches

**Research date:** 2026-08-26 (GMT+7)
**Repository branch:** `research/traditional-food-niches-2026`
**Status:** Working notes; not yet the final handoff.

## Scope

Riset ini mengevaluasi peluang niche visual stock/AI untuk makanan tradisional Indonesia dan kemungkinan ekspansi ke makanan ikonik negara lain. Result count marketplace diperlakukan sebagai sinyal supply, bukan bukti penjualan.

## Initial source findings

### Adobe Stock — Creating What’s In Demand
Source: https://stock.adobe.com/pages/artisthub/get-started/create-stock-content-that-sells-stock-contributor-guide-pt-1

Adobe advises contributors to select a niche, study buyers, analyze trends and supply/demand, use a mix of evergreen, seasonal, and trending content, and create content with a fresh perspective. Adobe explicitly lists food among evergreen themes and suggests combining food with durable trends such as sustainability or a local angle. Adobe also emphasizes commercial usability, realism, culturally representative content, and avoiding risk/controversy. The page says a blank Adobe Stock search can provide a real-time view of currently popular content and that contributors should inspect curated collections and contributor insights.

### Adobe Stock — Generative AI Content Guidelines
Source: https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/submit-your-content/submit-generative-ai-content/generative-ai-content-guidelines.html

Adobe requires contributors to have all necessary rights to submit AI-generated content for commercial licensing. Prompts, titles, and keywords must not include artist names, real people or fictional characters, copyrighted creative works, government agency names, third-party IP, or claims that imply a real newsworthy event. Identifiable people/property may require releases. All generative AI content must be marked as created using generative AI tools. For food-only objects with no recognizable people or property, releases are generally not required, but the policy and tool terms still need to be checked.

## Hypothesis implications

1. A food niche should be framed as a buyer-useful visual system, not just a list of dishes.
2. A strong first collection can combine evergreen isolated food objects with localized cultural details, but should avoid unverified claims of authenticity.
3. The safest initial production route is one food subject per asset, clean background, no visible text or brand, and multiple buyer use cases such as editorial, menu/packaging concept, tourism storytelling, and social content.
4. For AI-generated stock, metadata should use the dish name, region, ingredients, technique, cultural context, and visual descriptors only when supported by evidence.

## Validated findings from academic sources

### Wijaya (2019), Journal of Ethnic Foods
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42779-019-0009-3

The review describes Indonesian food culture as shaped by nature, history, and culture, and argues that promotion should communicate socio-cultural values rather than merely expose a long list of dishes. It notes uneven exposure, with Java and Sumatra receiving more attention than other regions. It describes three broad phases of culinary development: original/indigenous, multicultural influence through trade and colonisation, and contemporary adaptation. The article identifies ingredients, preparation, cooking, serving, utensils, and eating practices as visible cultural differentiators. Implication for visual niche design: regional dish + ingredient ecology + preparation/serving ritual is a stronger concept unit than a generic plate.

### Surya & Tedjakusuma (2022), Diversity of Sambals
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42779-022-00142-7

The review profiles 110 Indonesian sambal varieties gathered from cookbooks. It reports that Java contains 64.5% of identified variants, followed by Borneo 10.9%, Sulawesi 9.1%, and Bali/Nusa Tenggara/Maluku/Papua together 15.5%. More than 80% are prepared by crushing and cooking; the paper reports 82.7% cooked and 84.6% paste-like. Secondary ingredients create strong regional identity: fermented shrimp paste, fruits such as carambola/pineapple/mango/papaya, local fish, herbs, fermented foods, and kluwak. Examples with high visual differentiation include sambal andaliman (North Sumatra), sambal tempoyak (Bengkulu), sambal kluwak (East Java; dark appearance), sambal cakalang (North Sulawesi), and raw/chopped sambals from eastern regions. This supports a standalone condiment collection with strong colour/material/texture variation, but names and regional claims require careful validation.

### Wargadalem, Wasino & Yulifar (2023), Pempek Palembang
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42779-023-00209-z

The article presents pempek as a Palembang/South Sumatra identity built from fish, tapioca/sago, seasonings, and cuko sauce. The authors connect its development to the riverine ecology of South Sumatra, availability of fish and starch, Palembang history, and social practices such as Nginum gatherings. The research documents variation in fish, flour, shape, and production methods, and reports that pempek evolved from household preparation into a wider industry and a souvenir/identity product. Visual implication: pempek has a clear shape family, cross-section, dark cuko contrast, ingredient story, and product/packaging use cases; it is a promising pilot candidate if the imagery does not invent a single false canonical shape.

### Frez-Muñoz et al. (2021), Frontiers in Nutrition
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8415227/

The cross-sectional study surveyed 1,784 respondents across nine countries, including 172 respondents in Indonesia. It defines traditional foods/beverages as culturally connected, passed through generations, representative of local cuisine, known, and still consumed. The study used a flexible nationwide criterion for Indonesia because the country consists of many islands; comparable foods with different names could be grouped. In the overall study, younger cohorts more often mentioned snacks and on-the-go foods, while older cohorts more often mentioned time-consuming savoury preparations. The authors classify Indonesia among countries where younger cohorts C1/C2 clustered in the food-identity analysis. This supports testing both iconic meals and snack/portable formats, but the sample is from 2018 and is not a current marketplace demand measure.

### UNESCO list page
Source: https://ich.unesco.org/en/lists

The UNESCO browsing page provides the official list interface for intangible cultural heritage and explains that nominations, images/videos, committee decisions, and evidence of community consent can be explored. A UNESCO listing should be treated as heritage evidence for a practice, not as proof of stock-market demand or permission to reproduce a community symbol without context.
